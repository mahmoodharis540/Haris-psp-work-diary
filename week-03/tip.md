# Tips for this week

This week was focused on getting feedback on the work we had completed so far and using that feedback to improve the direction of the project. My main contribution was helping present our paper prototypes and early coded elements to Peter and Martin. I saw this as an important stage because it gave us a chance to test whether our initial ideas made sense before we became too committed to them in development.
During this session, I presented the wireframes we had been working from and also showed some of the code that had already started to translate those designs into a working structure. This was useful because it demonstrated that the project was beginning to move beyond planning and into practical implementation. It also allowed us to compare our original ideas with how they were actually functioning once built, which highlighted areas that were working well and areas that would need refinement.
The feedback we received was valuable because it helped clarify what needed to be improved in both the design and the development approach. Rather than treating the wireframes as final, I used the feedback as a way of identifying what needed to change so the application would better fit the project aims and user needs. This process also helped me better understand the importance of iterative development, where ideas are reviewed, tested, and improved instead of being accepted too early without challenge.

A key part of my contribution this week was helping the group respond constructively to that feedback. Instead of seeing the comments as criticism, I saw them as guidance that would help us produce a stronger outcome. This helped shape the next steps of development and gave us a clearer idea of where we needed to focus our time and effort going forward.
Overall, this week was important because it strengthened the project through review and refinement. Presenting our work to Peter and Martin helped confirm that we were making progress, while also revealing changes that would improve the final application. Looking back, this week showed me that feedback is one of the most useful parts of the development process, because it helps turn a basic early concept into something more realistic, usable, and professionally considered.
