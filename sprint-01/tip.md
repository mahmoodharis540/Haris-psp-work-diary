# Tips for this week

Sprint One was a major stage in the project because it moved our team from early planning into a more intensive period of development, coordination, and problem solving. My role during this sprint was not limited to one task, as I was involved in both the organisational side of the team and the technical development of the application itself. From the start of the sprint, I focused on helping the group work in a more structured way by supporting task allocation, keeping track of priorities, and making sure the team was progressing toward something we could confidently demonstrate.

One of my main contributions during Sprint One was helping organise the development process. I took on a role similar to a Scrum lead within the group by helping assign tasks, checking what different team members were working on, and trying to keep the work aligned with the agreed project goals. I found this important because sprint work can become disorganised very quickly if people are working in isolation or without a clear sense of priority. By helping coordinate responsibilities and maintain communication, I contributed to a more focused and productive team workflow.

Alongside this organisational role, I was also heavily involved in practical development work. During the sprint I worked on important areas of the application such as the login and admin-related pages, the settings page, accessibility-related features, and improvements to the visual consistency of the interface, including the NHS colour styling. I wanted the application to feel more complete and realistic as a working product rather than just a collection of disconnected pages. This meant that my work was not only about making things function technically, but also about helping shape the overall quality and usability of the system.

A significant challenge during Sprint One was dealing with technical issues that affected progress, particularly around development setup and integration. I spent time troubleshooting framework-related issues and helping deal with problems linked to technologies such as Next.js and Prisma. These issues were important to resolve because they could easily slow down the whole team if left unmanaged. I also supported the group by helping merge work, checking ideas with lecturers when needed, and making sure that blockers were discussed rather than ignored. This allowed us to keep moving forward even when parts of the project became more complex than expected.
Another important part of my contribution was helping prepare the team for what came after the sprint. As the week progressed, I was already thinking ahead to what still needed improvement and what our next priorities should be. This included helping shape the action plan for Sprint Two so that the team could build on what had been achieved rather than restarting its focus from scratch. I saw this as important because Sprint One was not just about producing immediate output, but also about learning what was still missing and how the second sprint could be used more effectively.

Overall, Sprint One demonstrated the importance of balancing leadership, technical work, and adaptability. My contribution covered planning, coding, troubleshooting, and team coordination, which helped the group make solid progress during a demanding development period. Looking back, this sprint showed me that successful software development depends not only on writing code, but also on keeping people organised, responding to problems quickly, and maintaining a clear sense of direction under pressure.
