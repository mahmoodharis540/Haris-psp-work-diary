# Tips for this week

One of the main things I reflected on after Sprint One was that we had made clear progress in areas such as the basic structure of the application, early accessibility work, and the setup of important pages such as login, settings, and staff-facing functionality. However, it was also clear that several areas still needed more refinement before the application could be considered ready for final delivery. In particular, there was a need to improve the overall integration of features, strengthen the route and path functionality, continue building accessibility support, and make sure the project was moving toward something that would be strong enough to present confidently to the client.
My role this week involved helping the team focus on what should come next rather than allowing effort to become too spread out. I contributed to discussions around task priorities and helped identify which features were most important to complete or improve before Sprint Two. This included thinking ahead about areas such as route reliability, interface quality, translations, staff-side functions, and the general stability of the application. I also considered how the work completed so far could be built on more effectively, so that the second sprint would be more productive and better organised.


This week was also important because it reinforced the value of planning based on evidence rather than assumptions. Sprint One had exposed some of the technical and organisational issues we needed to handle better, so the action plan for the next phase had to be more realistic and more focused. I saw this as a useful shift, because it meant the team could approach the next sprint with clearer responsibilities and a stronger understanding of what the project still needed.
Overall, my contribution this week helped support the transition from Sprint One into Sprint Two by helping shape a more focused and practical plan for development. Looking back, this week showed me that reviewing progress honestly is just as important as building features, because a good action plan can make the difference between steady improvement and wasted effort.
